# Social and individual information use in a supplemented resource landscape by an avian scavenger
Kaija Gahm, May Moreno, Shaked Finger, Marta Acácio Nili Anglister Gideon Vaadia, Orr Spiegel and Noa Pinter-Wollman

## Abstract
Social information use in animal foraging decisions has long been a topic of interest because of its central role in the ecologies of many animal populations and because of the evolutionary implications for the evolution of sociality. Theory suggests that social information use should be favored over individual information gathering when food resources are unpredictable and shareable. Most studies test this hypothesis at the species level or using theoretical models. Griffon vultures (Gyps fulvus) are obligate scavengers that rely on a variety of types of social information when searching for carcasses, a food source that is widely distributed and often spatially and temporally unpredictable. We studied a GPS-tracked population of griffon vultures to investigate how social information use relates to resource predictability. We hypothesized that reliance on social, rather than individual, information in finding food should vary within a population according to the relative spatiotemporal predictability of food resources. We predicted that individuals would use social information more for less predictable resources. Furthermore, we predicted that younger birds will rely more heavily on social information than adults because of their less extensive individual experience. Furthermore, we investigated how the population, and individuals, use different types of social information within the same landscape (following conspecifics from the roost or cueing in on flight behaviors of others around them), as well as individual information (e.g., memory and individual searching). Leveraging animal tracking technology, our work provides an empirical perspective on varied foraging strategies in heterogeneous landscapes, with implications for supplementary feeding management and the evolution of social behavior.
